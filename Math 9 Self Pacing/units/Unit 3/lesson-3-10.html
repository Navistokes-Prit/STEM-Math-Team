Lesson 3.10

Algebra Tiles

Represent positive and negative algebraic terms with tiles, model distribution, and connect tile models to polynomial operations.

Practice LevelFoundation
Quick Review

What the Tiles Represent

Positive and Negative Tiles

Different tile shapes represent 1, x, and x². Filled and outlined tiles are used to distinguish positive and negative values.

1,   x,   x2

A matching positive and negative tile form a zero pair.

Distribution with Tiles

A rectangular tile arrangement can model multiplication and expansion.

3(x + 2) = 3x + 6

Three groups of x + 2 produce three x-tiles and six unit tiles.

New Learning

Using Algebra Tiles

1

Translate tile counts into algebraic terms.

Count x² tiles, x tiles, and unit tiles separately, keeping track of positive and negative tiles.

2

Use zero pairs when opposite tiles appear.

One positive tile and one negative tile of the same type cancel to zero.

3

Connect the model to symbolic algebra.

Tile arrangements can represent expanding, factoring, and dividing simple polynomial expressions.

Worked ExampleExpand with Tiles
3(x - 2)
  1. Make three groups of x - 2.
  2. This gives three positive x-tiles and six negative unit tiles.
  3. Answer: 3x - 6.
Worked ExampleDivide with Tiles
(6x + 12) ÷ 3
  1. Split 6x + 12 into three equal groups.
  2. Each group contains 2x + 4.
  3. Answer: 2x + 4.

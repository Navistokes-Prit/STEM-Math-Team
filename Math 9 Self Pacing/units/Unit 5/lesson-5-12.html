Inequalities 1

Practice Level Foundation
Quick Review

Inequality Notation

Four key symbols

< means less than, > means greater than, ≤ means less than or equal to, and ≥ means greater than or equal to.

\(x\le4\) includes 4; \(x<4\) does not.

Open and closed circles

Use an open circle for < or > because the endpoint is excluded. Use a closed circle for ≤ or ≥ because the endpoint is included.

The arrow points toward all values that satisfy the inequality.
New Learning

Solve Inequalities

1

Solve like an equation at first.

Use inverse operations to isolate the variable.

2

Flip the sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative.

This is the crucial difference from solving equations.

3

Check a value from the solution region.

Substitute a convenient number that should work and, if useful, a number outside the region that should fail.

Worked ExampleNo sign flip
\(x-5>11.5\)
  1. Add 5 to both sides.
  2. x > 16.5.
  3. The endpoint is open and the number-line arrow points right.
Worked ExampleNegative coefficient
\(-5x>10\)
  1. Divide both sides by −5.
  2. Because we divided by a negative, flip > to <.
  3. So x < −2.

Lesson 5.04

Slope and y-intercept

Interpret slope as rise over run and identify the y-intercept of a linear graph.

Practice Level Foundation
Quick Review

Slope and y-intercept

Slope is a rate of change

Slope tells how much y changes when x changes. For two points, use rise ÷ run: change in y divided by change in x.

\(m=\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\).

The y-intercept

The y-intercept is the y-value where the line crosses the y-axis. At the y-axis, x = 0. In slope-intercept form it is called b.

In \(y=3x-4\), the y-intercept is \(-4\).
New Learning

Read a Line Efficiently

1

Choose two clear points.

Grid intersections are easiest. Avoid estimating between grid lines when exact points are available.

2

Count rise and run with signs.

Up is positive rise; down is negative rise. Right is positive run. A descending line has negative slope.

3

Read the intercept at x = 0.

Trace the line to the y-axis. The coordinate is (0, b), but the y-intercept value itself is b.

Worked ExamplePositive slope
Points \((0,-1)\) and \((2,3)\)
  1. Rise = \(3-(-1)=4\).
  2. Run = \(2-0=2\).
  3. So \(m=\frac{4}{2}=2\), and the y-intercept is −1.
Worked ExampleNegative slope
Points \((0,4)\) and \((3,1)\)
  1. Rise = \(1-4=-3\).
  2. Run = \(3-0=3\).
  3. So \(m=\frac{-3}{3}=-1\).

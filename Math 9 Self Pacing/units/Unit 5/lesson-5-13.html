Lesson 5.13

Inequalities 2

Solve inequalities with brackets or variables on both sides and represent real-world restrictions with inequalities.

Practice Level Foundation
Quick Review

More Complex Inequalities

Brackets work the same way

Use the distributive property exactly as you would in an equation. Keep the inequality sign unchanged unless you multiply or divide both sides by a negative.

\(5(x-4)<2(x+1)\).

Variables can appear on both sides

Collect variable terms on one side and constants on the other. The final sign depends on the algebra, especially any division by a negative coefficient.

\(6x+6>20x-5\).
New Learning

Solve, Represent, Interpret

1

Expand and simplify.

Distribute through brackets and combine like terms on each side.

2

Isolate the variable.

Track whether the final coefficient is positive or negative before dividing.

3

Represent the answer.

Write the inequality, show it on a number line when asked, and interpret it in words for a real-world problem.

Worked ExampleBrackets
\(5(x-4)<2(x+1)\)
  1. Expand: 5x − 20 < 2x + 2.
  2. Subtract 2x: 3x − 20 < 2.
  3. Add 20: 3x < 22, so x < 22/3.
Worked ExampleSign reversal
\(6x+6>20x-5\)
  1. Subtract 20x: −14x + 6 > −5.
  2. Subtract 6: −14x > −11.
  3. Divide by −14 and flip the sign: x < 11/14.
